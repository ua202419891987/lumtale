Why we built LumTale.

LumTale started with a frustrating observation: the apps we loved were quietly changing who we were. We built a fairer alternative — portraits that look like you, in a style that's genuinely our own.

The founding insight

A few years ago, one of our founders sent a childhood photo of her grandmother through a popular AI avatar app. The result was technically beautiful — and completely wrong. The warm, deep tones of her grandmother's skin had been lifted into something paler and "cleaner." The cheekbones had been narrowed. The face that came back looked less like the woman who raised her than like a generic template the app seemed to expect.

It wasn't a glitch. It was a pattern. Across most major avatar apps, non-white faces get quietly reshaped into something paler and more "standard." The models were trained mostly on light skin and Western features, so they nudged everyone toward that default. People of color, especially, paid a hidden cost: their portraits looked less like themselves and more like a flattened approximation of someone else.

We refused that. Identity is not a bug to be smoothed away. A portrait app should celebrate the face in front of it, not correct it toward an imagined norm. That conviction became the first line of LumTale's code and the last line of every paragraph on this site.

The birth of the Lumling style

Once we knew what we were against, we had to decide what we were for. We didn't want a flat filter, and we didn't want to imitate the look of any single studio or artist. So we designed our own: Lumling — a warm, collectible-toy portrait style with soft rounded shapes, gentle earthy colors, and a friendly, handmade feel.

Lumling is a new visual language we created for LumTale. It is explicitly not a copy of any existing artist, franchise, or brand. We built it from scratch so that people who use LumTale get something that feels fresh and ours, while their real features — skin tone, eye shape, bone structure — stay unmistakably theirs. A Lumling portrait should make you think "that's me as a tiny treasured keepsake," not "that's a stock character."

Today Lumling lives in a few flavors: Lumling Dream for a dream-painted finish, Lumling Toon for a brighter cartoon look, and Lumling Studio for the deepest, most detailed renders. More are on the way.

Our two-door philosophy

Privacy isn't one-size-fits-all, so we built two honest ways in. You pick the door that fits the moment — and you always know which one you chose.

Door 1 — Local

Your device, your photo

Door 1 runs the model right in your browser using WebGPU. Your photo never leaves your machine, is never uploaded, and is never used to train anything. It's the most private way to get a Lumling portrait — and it's the door we point privacy-first users to first.

Door 2 — Cloud

Honest, premium rendering

Door 2 sends your photo to a vetted GPU partner for a higher-quality Lumling render. We're upfront about it: you'll see exactly where your photo goes before you upload, we link the partner's data policy, and we never store or train on your image. Honesty over fine print.

Our promise to your face

Everything above comes down to three commitments we will not break. They aren't marketing lines — they're the rules our models and our code are built to honor.

No whitening

We don't lift or lighten your skin tone toward some "standard" shade. Your color is your color.

No warping

We don't narrow your features or reshape your bone structure. Your face stays your face.

No training on you

We never store your photo or train our models on it — local or cloud.

What's next

LumTale is early, and that's the exciting part. Here's a small, honest look at where we're headed:

  • More Lumling styles — seasonal palettes and a soft "Lumling Classic" line for everyday profile pictures.
  • Faster Door 1 — shrinking the on-device model so private portraits render in seconds, even on laptops.
  • Batch packs — turn a handful of photos into a matching Lumling set for the whole family.
  • Transparency dashboard — a simple page showing exactly what each door does with your photo, in plain language.
